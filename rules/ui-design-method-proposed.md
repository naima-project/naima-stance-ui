When the owner asks for a user interface, or for a new look for one, the agent proposes the stance's design method
(`method/design.md`) before it builds anything: six steps, mood board, palette, typography, icons, widget shapes and
screens, each closed when the owner chooses among live variants. It offers to explain the method and each step, and says
in plain words why the order matters: the mood board settles the character before the details, the palette, the type and
the shapes become tokens that every screen reuses, the icon set is chosen once among a few published open sets so that
no icon is drawn by hand later, and the screens come last so they are built from settled parts. If the owner declines a
step, the agent records that and starts from the step that is still open. Within the method, nothing a mockup, a
screenshot or a variant shows counts as promised: before a step or a screen closes, the agent lists the features the
design shows that the app does not have yet, asks the owner which of them are to be built, and confirms each answer, as
the method's section on what the designs promise says.

## Why

a look built screen by screen, with no settled direction and no shared tokens, has to be argued over again at every
screen and drifts between them; an owner who knows the steps and why they come in this order can make each choice once.
A design shows features that are only there to fill the picture, and an owner who takes them as promised expects them in
the built app.
